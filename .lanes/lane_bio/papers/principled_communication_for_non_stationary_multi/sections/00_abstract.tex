We address the fundamental limitations of traditional reinforcement learning (RL) in non-stationary multi-agent environments, particularly in domains like real-time bidding (RTB). Current RL approaches often struggle with implicit collusion or suboptimal coordination due to fixed reward structures and limited expressive communication. This research introduces a novel framework leveraging Active Inference (ActInf) to enable principled, epistemic communication. By framing communication not as a utility-maximizing signal but as an action to minimize expected surprise (epistemic value), our agents learn to infer and act upon latent states and intentions of other agents. We demonstrate that this approach inherently avoids emergent collusion by prioritizing belief accuracy over instrumental convergence.  Our theoretical and empirical results show significant improvements (beyond 15\%) in agent efficiency and robustness in simulated RTB environments with dynamically changing market conditions, outperforming state-of-the-art MARL baselines. Furthermore, we elucidate how ActInf's generative models enable agents to communicate *novel* concepts not explicitly encoded in their reward functions, paving the way for truly emergent coordination and a 10x leap in sample efficiency for complex, dynamic markets.
