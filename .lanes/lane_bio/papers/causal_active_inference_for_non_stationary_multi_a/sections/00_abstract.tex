Real-time bidding (RTB) markets exemplify the challenges of non-stationarity, adverse selection, and structural information asymmetry, rendering traditional reinforcement learning and game theory approaches insufficient for achieving true market equilibrium. This research proposes Causal Active Inference (CAI) as a novel paradigm to address these limitations. CAI integrates variational principles with causal discovery, enabling agents not merely to adapt to but to actively shape the market environment by inferring and acting upon underlying causal structures. Unlike existing methods that approximate Nash equilibria or optimize within fixed utility functions, CAI posits that agents can leverage their internal generative models to anticipate and influence the emergent causal relationships governing market dynamics, including the behavior of other agents. We demonstrate through rigorous theoretical analysis and controlled simulations that CAI can predict and induce state transitions in market equilibrium that are otherwise inaccessible. Our approach tackles the 'missing reward' problem not as an epistemic deficit but as a consequence of unobserved causal factors, which CAI's active inference framework is designed to uncover. This reframing offers a path towards understanding and potentially engineering more stable and efficient market mechanisms, extending beyond ad-tech to domains such as financial markets and resource allocation.
